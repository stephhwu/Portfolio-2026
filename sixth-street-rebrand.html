A ground-up identity rebuild for a global investment firm.

Services

Brief

Sixth Street is a global investment firm working across the full spectrum of company growth — building businesses, financing them, and identifying value where others don't look. I joined their site redesign to build out the design system, then redesigned their logo into a mark built entirely from equilateral triangles, forming a six with a diamond at its center. That motif became the throughline for the rest of the identity — I helped carry it through the brand guidelines and a scalable set of bespoke components.

Finding

The original mark had uneven visual weight — inconsistent angles that never quite balanced. Rebuilding it from a grid of identical equilateral triangles gave the six, and everything built from it, a geometric logic flexible enough to become icons, dividers, and data visualization without ever looking arbitrary.

Overview

The mark behind "Prismatic Thinking."

Sixth Street's new hero headline, "Prismatic Thinking," needed a logo that lived up to it. The mark is built entirely from triangles of the same size and shape, arranged into a six with a diamond at its center — sharp angles on the outside, softened just enough to feel confident rather than aggressive.

Detail

A pattern built to reuse.

This case-study carousel is one of the scalable components built for the new design system — a pattern that could hold any number of investments, in any order, without needing a bespoke layout every time.

Process

From an uneven mark to a full system.

The existing logo had real balance problems — the geometry didn't sit right at any size. I rebuilt it from scratch: a six made entirely of equal triangles, with a diamond at the center where they meet. That same triangle logic — sharp angles, softened edges — carried through the rest of the identity, and the diamond became a recurring component across the site. I supported the brand guidelines alongside the broader design system update, documenting how the new mark and its motifs should scale across every touchpoint — down to a single motion cue built around "One prism, multiple perspectives."
