Brief

A qualitative companion to Beneath the Surface, built to bring attention to coral reefs using open-access images and research from the Smithsonian's Invertebrate Zoology department. Corals aid in cancer research and carry anti-viral properties, and they're a crucial habitat for fish. But the deeper-water corals in this data are increasingly damaged by ocean trawling, which became a focal point of the story.

Finding

We aimed for a minimalistic, vintage illustrative aesthetic to harmonize the research with the qualitative data points, and guided users through with a scrollytelling structure: a progress bar that encourages discovery rather than dictating pace.

Why It Matters

A minimalist, vintage aesthetic for an urgent story.

Corals are a crucial building block for many ocean depths, yet the ones surviving in deeper, colder water are being damaged by trawling practices most people never see. The illustrative, vintage treatment was a deliberate choice. It's a quieter register than the usual climate-crisis imagery, meant to hold the reader's attention on the research itself.
